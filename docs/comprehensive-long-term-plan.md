# Comprehensive long-term plan
## Troy Goodfellow — April 2026

Status: Draft. Revisit quarterly. Update annually with year-end reflection.

This plan covers four interconnected threads:
1. **WardForge** — software business
2. **Creator network** — aligned media infrastructure
3. **Connective tissue** — coordination layer for aligned organizations
4. **Think tank / NGO** — research and advocacy operation

These are deliberately sequenced. Each phase builds on the previous.
Skipping ahead means the later phases fail.

---

## Strategic principles

These hold across all four threads:

**1. WardForge first.** The software business generates resources,
credibility, technology, relationships, and data infrastructure that
make everything else possible. Distraction from WardForge in early years
undermines everything downstream.

**2. Behind the scenes.** No public partisan identity until at minimum
2029-2030. WardForge stays nonpartisan publicly. Personal political work
is private. Plan for eventual public scrutiny.

**3. Compounding over speed.** Political infrastructure takes years to
build credibility. Don't rush. The right work in 2030 beats fast work
in 2027.

**4. Resources before influence.** Generate capital, talent, and
relationships through legitimate work first. Apply to political work
later. Premature ambition destroys both.

**5. Founder/business separation.** WardForge serves customers regardless
of politics. Personal political work happens through separate vehicles.
Even when both succeed, they remain operationally and legally distinct.

**6. Discipline of focus.** The temptation to start political work
prematurely is real. Resist. Focus is the multiplier; fragmentation kills.

## Major risks and contingencies

### Risk 1: WardForge fails or stalls

If WardForge doesn't reach profitable growth by 2028-2029, the political
infrastructure ambitions become much harder to fund and execute.

Contingency: Compress political infrastructure timeline or wait. Don't
sacrifice WardForge focus to chase political work prematurely.

### Risk 2: Public exposure of behind-the-scenes work

Sometime between 2028-2032, journalists will probably reconstruct your
political infrastructure work. Plan for this.

Contingency: Have a clear narrative ready. The honest version is:
"I founded a software company that serves all campaigns. Personally,
I've supported various aligned projects through normal civic
participation. There's no contradiction." This is defensible if true.

### Risk 3: Conservative political environment shifts unfavorably

If Canadian conservative momentum stalls or reverses, the political
infrastructure work becomes less viable.

Contingency: WardForge continues independently. Political infrastructure
work waits or redirects.

### Risk 4: Founder burnout or change of heart

Six to nine years is long. You might not want this in 2030.

Contingency: Plan for graceful handoffs. WardForge should have a CEO
that isn't you by Phase 3 if you want to scale political work. Political
infrastructure should have multiple leaders, not be founder-dependent.

### Risk 5: Matthew alignment fails

Co-founder dynamics over a 9-year arc are non-trivial. You and Matthew
need to stay aligned or have a clean exit path for one of you.

Contingency: Establish co-founder agreements that handle dispute
resolution and exit scenarios. Have honest yearly conversations about
trajectory and commitment.

### Risk 6: Funding doesn't materialize

VC funding for political tech is uncertain. Philanthropic capital for
political infrastructure is competitive.

Contingency: Bootstrap longer if needed. Adjust ambition to available
capital. Don't take bad money to maintain ambitious timeline.

### Risk 7: Legal or regulatory exposure

Political work has legal complexity. Election finance laws, third-party
advertising rules, lobbying registration requirements.

Contingency: Legal counsel from Phase 2 onward. Conservative
interpretation of regulations. Don't let ambition push into gray areas.

### Risk 8: Operational complexity exceeds capacity

Running four interconnected operations is hard. Most attempts fail.

Contingency: Be willing to drop or pause individual threads if they
threaten the others. WardForge first; everything else is conditional.

## What this plan is not

A few honest acknowledgments:

This is not a guaranteed path. Most ambitious multi-year plans fail or
substantially redirect. Treat this as a working hypothesis to be revised
with evidence.

This is not a substitute for execution. The plan is the easy part.
Doing the work over years is the hard part.

This is not the only possible path. If WardForge succeeds wildly,
you might focus there exclusively. If political work calls strongly,
you might exit WardForge and focus there. The plan is the integrated
maximalist version; reality may force narrower focus.

This is not a commitment to all four threads forever. Drop any thread
that isn't working. Don't sunk-cost into something that's not
producing.

## Closing principle

The most important thing across all four threads: **be successful at
the current phase before moving to the next.**

WardForge in 2026 is the work. Political infrastructure observation
and relationship building in 2027-2028 is the work. First political
infrastructure experiments in 2029 are the work.

Each phase has a job. Trust the sequence. The maximalist vision is
achievable only if each phase succeeds before the next begins.

Don't let the long-term vision distract from short-term execution.
The vision is what you're working toward; the work in front of you
is how you get there.
