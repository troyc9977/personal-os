# 0002: Priority ordering of life commitments

## Status

Accepted — 2026-05-03

## Context

Without an explicit priority order, life commitments compete invisibly for the same hour. The pattern in many lives — including mine, recently — is that whichever commitment generates the loudest urgency wins, regardless of whether it is the one that should win.

After review, the actual order I want to live by is different from what casual observation might suggest. WardForge is the dominant work, but family and friends sit higher than reading, contemplation, writing, and DCC — not because they consume more time per week, but because of what I would lose if they slipped.

## Decision

The binding priority order is:

1. **WardForge** — primary work-life vehicle through 2029.
2. **Family and friends** — the people I am loyal to.
3. **Reading** — interior formation.
4. **Health** — running, gym, sleep, stretching.
5. **Contemplative practice** — currently re-anchoring at a 10-min daily floor.
6. **Writing** — Substack, longer pieces, the decline-of-the-West essay.
7. **DCC** — current employment, ~1 year remaining target.

When two commitments compete for the same hour, the higher one wins. The order is binding until explicitly revised in writing through a new ADR.

Notable choices in this ordering:

- **Family and friends above reading.** A deliberate reversal of how my time has actually been allocated. Time with loved ones beats time with books.
- **Reading above contemplation.** Reading is currently doing some of the interior work formal contemplation used to do. Both stay on the list; reading wins on time, contemplation has its own protected morning slot.
- **Writing below WardForge specifically because it gets less weekly time, not because it is less important.** Writing is still a long-arc commitment.
- **DCC last.** It is a means to an end with about a year left in it.

## Consequences

### Positive
- Decisions about how to spend a given evening or weekend become tractable. The list resolves it.
- Drift becomes detectable. If I am consistently choosing #6 over #2, that is a signal worth surfacing.
- The order can be referenced in weekly reviews and by the brain (when deployed) to flag misalignment.

### Negative
- Rigidity risk. Real life has weeks where #4 (health) genuinely needs to outrank #1 (WardForge) — illness, injury, recovery. The order is a default, not a law of physics. Override is allowed; silent drift is not.
- The ordering is contestable. Some readers (or future-me) might reasonably argue that contemplative practice should be higher than #5. The honest answer right now is that contemplation is being recovered from a slip, and I am not yet in a position to put it above health or reading. Revisit in 3 months.

### Neutral
- The order will be revised. ADRs are not sacred; they are records of decisions that can be superseded with explicit reasoning.

## Alternatives considered

- **No formal ordering, "just use judgment."** Rejected. This is what I have been doing, and it has produced exactly the drift this ADR is designed to fix.
- **Equal priority for everything except WardForge.** Rejected. False humility. Some things actually matter more than others, and pretending otherwise produces worse decisions, not better ones.
- **Contemplation higher.** Considered. Rejected for now because contemplation is currently slipped and putting it at #2 or #3 without an actual practice would be aspirational rather than honest. Once the 10-minute floor holds for a few months, this ordering gets revisited.
