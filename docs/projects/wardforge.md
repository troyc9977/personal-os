# WardForge

**Status**: active, primary work-life vehicle through 2029.

This file is the *personal-context* view of WardForge. It is not the operational substrate — that lives in the WardForge repo. This file captures what WardForge is to me, where it stands at a high level, and what it's costing me in time, attention, and capacity.

Last updated: 2026-05-03

## What it is

Co-founded with Matthew. Toronto-based political technology company. Phase 1 product (RidingPulse) launches into Ontario's October 26, 2026 municipal elections. Phase 2 expands to provincial, federal, and a permissioned political intelligence network for campaigns, slates, NGOs, donors, and aligned organizations. Full operational detail in `Campaign-OS/wardforge-web` repo.

## Where it sits in my long-arc

Technology cylinder of the 25-year project. The vehicle through which I do "build a Canadian software company that stays Canadian." A 5-10 year build, not a 3-year flip.

## What "winning" Phase 1 actually looks like

Not the founder-deck answer. The honest one:

- **Minimum**: one real user. A campaign manager who actively uses the product through October 26.
- **What I am actually optimizing for**: learning how to run a startup. Putting the effort in. Building momentum that compounds into Phase 2 regardless of Phase 1's commercial outcome.
- **What I am not optimizing for**: a launch-week press hit, a Series A by Q1 2027, or hitting an arbitrary user count.

If WardForge ships on October 26 with one real user and I have learned how to run a startup, that is success. If it ships with five users and I have not learned, that is a different kind of outcome.

## What I am worried about

Honest at-2am answers, not founder-deck risks.

- **Not finding users.** The single most named risk. Customer outreach is the area I most under-invest in, and it is the binding constraint on Phase 1 success.
- **Founder coordination with Matthew under launch pressure.**
- **Voters' list ingestion (Sep 1) being harder than estimated.**
- **Customer development under-investing because building feels more productive (it is not).**
- **Personal capacity if DCC + WardForge both spike at the same time.**

## What I suspect might be the wrong work

This is the secret-suspect category. Real founders have these and don't say them out loud.

- **Possibly under-investing in user outreach.** I keep deferring it for "after this next build sprint." That pattern is exactly how startups fail to find product-market fit.
