# Substack / Writing Practice

**Status**: in development. Public launch September-October 2026.

Last updated: 2026-05-03

## What it is

Personal Substack. Biweekly cadence. The intellectual through-line for the public-facing work — combining DCC practitioner experience, WardForge build context, and broader reading on Canadian institutions, infrastructure, and modernization.

## Positioning

I am building toward being Canada's foremost voice on infrastructure delivery and political modernization — the practitioner-intellectual lane that almost nobody currently occupies in Canada. Most infrastructure writing is academic, opaque, or partisan. The opportunity is direct, technically literate, civically serious writing for an educated general audience.

*Comment Magazine* is the first target outside-publication.

## Reading anchor

- Bent Flyvbjerg, *How Big Things Get Done* — the master text.
- Infrastructure Ontario frameworks.
- Auditor General reports (Ontario and federal).
- Caro on Robert Moses (different era, same animal).

## Themes I'm developing

- How big things actually get built (and why so few of them in Canada now).
- Productivity decline and capital misallocation in Canadian housing/real estate.
- The 52 GTA infrastructure authorities by domain and jurisdiction — *(research from earlier work)*.
- Political modernization without losing what's worth keeping.
- The contemplative roots of serious public work.

## Cadence

- **Now → September 2026**: research, drafting, building backlog.
- **September-October 2026**: public launch with backlog of 4-6 essays already written.
- **Post-launch**: biweekly posts, 1500-3000 words, one publication-target essay every quarter.

## What I am NOT writing

- Daily political commentary.
- Hot takes on the news cycle.
- Anything that requires being chronically online.

## Discipline

- Writing time: Saturday mornings primarily, Sunday afternoons as needed.
- Drafts in Obsidian / a separate writing repo, finished posts published on Substack.
- A piece is finished when it's true and well-said, not when it's long enough.
