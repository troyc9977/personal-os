# Heritage Restoration Acquisition

**Status**: thesis-stage. Active acquisition target window: 2028-2029.

Last updated: 2026-05-03

## What it is

The plan to acquire a small Toronto/Ontario heritage restoration business as a cash-flow anchor by 2028-2029. Cylinder for the *beauty* lane of the 25-year project — and a connection to physical-craft work that software-only life lacks.

## Why this specifically

- Heritage restoration is an undermechanized, undermanaged, owner-operator-aging sector with real demand.
- Toronto and Ontario have a deep stock of heritage buildings that need long-term ongoing work.
- Buyers are scarce; sellers have few good exit options. Asymmetric in favor of patient, well-financed acquirers.
- The work is culturally meaningful — actual buildings, actual craft, actual physical presence in the city — in a way that pure software is not.
- It pairs with the larger civilizational project: beauty as actual built environment, not abstraction.

## Why not now

- Capital not yet sufficient.
- Operating bandwidth fully claimed by WardForge through 2026, partially through 2027.
- Better to go in with WardForge as a stable cash-flow cylinder of its own and partial liquidity from any future raise/exit.

## What I'm doing in the meantime

- Reading the small-business acquisition literature (Stanley Tang, Walker Deibel, etc.) — slowly, no rush.
- Watching the Ontario heritage restoration market peripherally. Building a mental model of who the operators are.
- Building credibility in adjacent worlds (DCC infrastructure experience, future Substack writing on built environment) that will matter when I am ready to approach owners.

## What this is NOT

- A pivot from WardForge.
- A get-rich-quick scheme via SBA loans and search funds.
- A "quit my job and start a holding company" fantasy.

It is a patient, multi-year cylinder that activates when WardForge is mature. Treat accordingly.

## Decision triggers

This thesis becomes active work when:

1. WardForge is at $2M+ ARR with clear path to $5M.
2. Personal capital position can support a meaningful down payment + working capital.
3. I have an Ontario commercial banker relationship and a heritage-restoration-savvy lawyer lined up.

Until those three are true, this stays a thesis.
