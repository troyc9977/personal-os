# Substack writing under Troy Campbell — 2026

## Why this matters

The shape of this goal changed materially on 2026-05-12 with ADR-0014.
Writing and RidingPulse used to be separate workstreams pointing at
different timelines. They are now one coupled motion: the writing is the
audience-led GTM for the platform, and the platform is the analytical
engine for the writing. Under that coupling, writing is no longer a
priority-6 slot competing with DCC for evening time — it is part of the
priority-2 RidingPulse bucket. The priority change from 6 to 2 in this
file reflects that.

This also supersedes the timeline portion of ADR-0009, which set a
November 2026 target keyed to external-venue acceptance (Comment
Magazine). The external-venue ambition is not abandoned — it is no longer
the gate. Getting the first piece out under my own byline on 2026-06-30,
paired with a working tool, beats waiting for an editor's yes.

Publication name on Substack: TBD, default to **Troy Campbell**. The
byline is mine; the analytical credibility is mine. The tool is the
artifact of the analyst's work, not the other way around.

## Approach

**Cadence**: piece 1 lands 2026-06-30, then biweekly through year-end.
The biweekly target gets re-validated after piece 1 ships and the actual
time-cost of publication-grade analysis is known. If weekly is sustainable,
upgrade then; if biweekly proves heavy, that becomes the honest answer.

**First piece (June 30)**: a specific lobbying case, anchored on the
RidingPulse tool's first usable output (network analysis across the
Toronto and Federal lobbying registries). The piece exists because the
tool exists — the visualizations are not decoration, they are the
analytical core. If the tool slips, the piece slips. If the piece slips
without the tool slipping, the writing discipline has failed and that is
the diagnosis to write down.

**Saturday is the primary writing block; Sunday afternoon is the overflow
slot.** Twitter cross-posts and real-time commentary on writing days. The
X following is now a deliberate audience-building motion paired with the
publication, not a separate growth project — the same time produces two
outputs.

A piece is finished when it's true and well-said, not when it's long
enough.

## Positioning

Building toward being Canada's foremost voice on infrastructure delivery
and political modernization — the practitioner-intellectual lane that
almost nobody currently occupies in Canada. Most infrastructure writing
is academic, opaque, or partisan. The opportunity is direct, technically
literate, civically serious writing for an educated general audience.

*Comment Magazine* remains the first outside-publication target if and
when external-venue submission becomes the right move. Held as a future
option, not a current gate.

## Themes

The range of pieces this publication develops over time:

- Lobbying networks and influence flows in Canadian politics — the
  RidingPulse tool's natural home and the angle for piece 1.
- How big things actually get built (and why so few of them in Canada now).
- Productivity decline and capital misallocation in Canadian housing /
  real estate.
- The 52 GTA infrastructure authorities by domain and jurisdiction.
- Political modernization without losing what's worth keeping.
- The contemplative roots of serious public work.
- The Decline-of-West essay as a longer arc threading through several
  pieces (parked; see Approach above).

## Reading anchor

- Bent Flyvbjerg, *How Big Things Get Done* — the master text on
  infrastructure delivery.
- Infrastructure Ontario frameworks.
- Auditor General reports (Ontario and federal).
- Caro on Robert Moses (different era, same animal).
- For the Decline-of-West thread when it reopens: Spengler, Burnham,
  MacIntyre, contemporary Canadian / British / American conservative
  thought.

## What this is not

- Daily political commentary.
- Hot takes on the news cycle.
- Anything that requires being chronically online.

**Decline-of-West essay parked as future piece.** Not the right opener
for this arc — the first piece needs to make what the RidingPulse tool
does visible. Decline-of-West is a thematic essay without a tool-output
hook, and shipping it first sells the wrong frame for the platform.
Existing work (thesis + 5-section outline captured in May daily entries)
is preserved. The essay is held in rotation and gets its turn once the
tool-anchored opening pieces have established what the publication is.
Discipline when re-engaged: open a document, write the worst version of
the first paragraph, and iterate from there. Starting badly is the
practice; starting well is the eventual outcome.

## Watch-for signals

- **Tool work eating writing time.** The pivot couples the two, but the
  failure mode is "I'll write once the tool is better." The tool is always
  going to be better next week. Piece 1 ships on the working output of
  2026-06-21, not on the polished one.
- **Case-shopping past the May 17 kickoff.** The point of locking the
  specific lobbying case is to start the research, not to optimize the
  case selection. By May 17, pick and commit.
- **Saturday writing block missed two weeks running without an explicit
  reason.** The whole goal runs through that one block; losing it is
  losing the goal.
- **Drafting without finishing through June.** Drafting forever is a
  known failure mode for writers like me. At some point a piece has to
  be true and well-said and shipped, even if not perfect.
- **Spending writing time on substrate / Compass / ADRs instead of prose
  or analysis.** The substrate-as-avoidance pattern from `CLAUDE.md`
  applies directly. The writing block is for prose and analysis.
- **Cadence collapsing after piece 1.** The biweekly cadence proves the
  publication is a ritual, not a single-piece launch. If piece 2 (July 14)
  doesn't land within a few days of target, the diagnosis is "is biweekly
  the wrong cadence?" — and that diagnosis is honest, not failure.

## Open

- **Publication name on Substack.** Default to "Troy Campbell." A
  publication-style name (e.g. something thematic about Canadian political
  analytics) is possible but not required for piece 1. Decide at the
  Substack page-setup milestone (2026-06-28).
- **Twitter strategy specifics.** Tone, cross-post timing, whether to run
  thread excerpts of pieces or stand-alone commentary in between. Lock at
  the same 2026-06-28 milestone.
- **External-venue submission.** Whether to layer Comment Magazine or
  peer publications as follow-up submissions for selected pieces.
  Decide post-publication of piece 1, with real engagement data in hand.
- **Decline-of-West essay placement.** Held in rotation. Reopen for
  scheduling once 3-4 tool-anchored pieces have shipped and the publication's
  shape is established.
